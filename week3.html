Bézout's Identity

Bézout's Identity
Let $a$ and $b$ be integers with greatest common divisor $d$. Then there exist integers $x$ and $y$ such that \[ ax + by = d. \] The integers $x$ and $y$ are called Bézout coefficients.

Find integers $x$ and $y$ such that $5x + 7y = 1$. $5(3) + 7(-2) = 1$.

Can you find any other solutions?

Proof of Bézout's Identity

Let set $S = \{ax + by \mid x, y \in \mathbb{Z} \text{ and } ax+by>0 \}$. Intuitively, this is the set of all positive integers that are an integer combination of $a$ and $b$.

This set is non-empty, since it contains either $a$ or $-a$. Hence a minimum element $d=as+bt$ exists.

We wish to prove $d = \gcd(a, b)$ (think about why this is desirable).

To do this, we need to prove two things:

  1. $d \mid a$ and $d \mid b$
  2. Any other common divisor $c$ satisfies $c \leq d$.

Proof of Bézout's Identity

Apply the division algorithm to $a$ by $d$: \[ a = qd + r, \quad 0 \leq r < d. \]

Rearranging the equation to solve for $r$: $$ \begin{aligned} r & = a - qd \\ & = a - q(as+bt) \\ & = a(1-qs) + b(-qt) \end{aligned} $$

This shows $r$ is an integer combination of $a$ and $b$, so $r \in S$ or $r = 0$.

But $d$ is the minimum element of $S$ and $r < d$, so $r$ cannot be in $S$. Hence $r = 0$, implying $d \mid a$.

Proof of Bézout's Identity

A similar argument shows that $d \mid b$ as well. Thus $d \mid a$ and $d \mid b$.

Now, let $c$ be any common divisor of $a$ and $b$. Then $a = cx$ and $b = cy$ for some integers $x$ and $y$. $$ \begin{aligned} d & = as + bt \\ & = cxs + cyt \\ & = c(xs + yt) \end{aligned} $$

This shows $c \mid d$. As $d > 0$, we have $c \leq d$.

Inverses (again)

We learned about this result (without proof) last class:

Existence of Inverses
An integer $a$ has a multiplicative inverse modulo $m$ if and only if $\gcd(a, m) = 1$.

We are now in a position to prove both directions!

1. Inverse exists implies $\gcd(a, m) = 1$.

This implies $a^{-1}$ exists, with $aa^{-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{m}$. Thus there exists an integer $k$ s.t. $aa^{-1} = 1 + km \implies aa^{-1} - km = 1$

Let $d = \gcd(a, m)$. Since $d \mid a$ and $d \mid m$, by the fact that gcd divides integer combination, we have $d \mid 1$. Therefore $d = 1$.

Inverses (again)

2. $\gcd(a, m) = 1$ implies inverse exists.

By Bézout's identity, there exist integers $x$ and $y$ such that $ax + my = 1$. Taking mod $m$ on both sides, we get $ax \equiv 1 \pmod{m}$, so $x$ is the multiplicative inverse of $a$ modulo $m$.

We have now succesfully proven both directions!

Note: The fact that we can find inverses by computing the Bézout coefficients will allow us to efficiently compute modular inverses.

Chinese Remainder Theorem

You have probably heard of systems of equations before. We can also have systems of modular congruences: $$ \begin{aligned} x &\equiv a_1 \pmod{m_1} \\ x &\equiv a_2 \pmod{m_2} \\ &\vdots \\ x &\equiv a_n \pmod{m_n} \end{aligned} $$

The Chinese Remainder Theorem guarantees that if the moduli $m_1, m_2, \dots, m_n$ are pairwise coprime, then the system has a unique solution modulo $m_1 m_2 \cdots m_n$.

Chinese Remainder Theorem

For small systems, we can solve them by just guess and check: \[ \begin{aligned} x &\equiv 2 \pmod{3} \\ x &\equiv 1 \pmod{4} \end{aligned} \]

What is the unique solution to this system? $x \equiv 5 \pmod{12}$.

For larger systems, we can use the method of successive substitutions to find a solution systematically: \[ \begin{aligned} x &\equiv 1 \pmod{3} \\ x &\equiv 4 \pmod{5} \\ x &\equiv 6 \pmod{7} \end{aligned} \]

Answer: $x \equiv 34 \pmod{105}$

Chinese Remainder Theorem

What are the last two digits of $49^{19}$?

We want to compute $x \equiv 49^{19} \pmod{100}$. Since $100 = 4 \cdot 25$ and $\gcd(4, 25) = 1$, the Chinese Remainder Theorem tells us that the solution for $x$ corresponds to solving this system: $$ \begin{aligned} x &\equiv 49^{19} \pmod{4} \\ x &\equiv 49^{19} \pmod{25} \end{aligned} $$

Answer: $x \equiv 49 \pmod{100}$

Chinese Remainder Theorem

Show that there exist $99$ consecutive integers, each of which is divisible by a perfect cube greater than $1$.

Consider this system of congruences: $$ \begin{aligned} x & \equiv -1 \pmod{p_1^3} \\ x & \equiv -2 \pmod{p_2^3} \\ & \vdots \\ x & \equiv -99 \pmod{p_{99}^3} \end{aligned} $$ where $p_i$ is the $i$-th prime number.

Since primes are coprime to each other, CRT guarantees this system has a solution. The $99$ integers are $\{x+1, x+2, \ldots, x+99\}$.
